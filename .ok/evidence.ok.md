# Claims, correspondence, and evidence

Draft for discussion. Not an activated policy or authorization to act.

Scope: claims made across code, documentation, manuscript sources, paper artifacts, issues, and reviews. This file owns what evidence establishes, not where work lives.

### evidence-01
Every new verification or acceptance claim identifies the exact evaluated artifact revision or hash, relevant comparison baseline, spec revision, method or command, outcome, and evidence location. A PR review also identifies the evaluated head and base; an artifact-only revalidation identifies its complete relevant dependency set.

Check: inspect the claim's evidence record or its explicitly inherited run context. A timestamp or branch name alone is insufficient revision identity. Historical records predating these specs retain their real provenance; missing metadata is disclosed, not fabricated retroactively.

### evidence-02
A cross-artifact correspondence claim compares the actual statements, domains, hypotheses, units or normalization, and quantity definitions at the named revisions. Matching theorem names, equation numbers, or successful compilation alone are not correspondence evidence.

Check: a reviewable mapping or narrowly scoped argument. Historical equation maps remain explicitly historical until regenerated and checked against current inputs.

### evidence-03
Reported conclusions distinguish elaboration, proof completeness, declared/imported axiom dependencies, regression coverage, manuscript consistency, and empirical validation. Evidence for one is not reported as evidence for the others; a conditional theorem is not reported as a derivation of its hypotheses.

Check: inspect result wording and cited checks. An absent proof is not a counterexample, and an admitted axiom is not thereby false or established.

### evidence-04
Every observation is recorded as pass, fail, or unknown, with its scope and reason; unknown is never presented as a pass or as a refutation of the underlying mathematics. Missing, expired, inaccessible, or cancelled evidence leaves the current claim unknown until a valid replacement exists. Superseded evidence keeps its original historical result; its applicability to a new subject is unknown until rechecked or justified carry-forward exists.

Check: the observation record. The ideal assertions themselves remain binary; these are epistemic observation labels, not a new DotOK task-status schema.

### evidence-05
An acceptance claim is supported by the agreed criteria for the delivered scope. A changed criterion has a visible authorized rationale and preserves remaining obligations; a disclaimer, renamed symbol, closed checkbox, or successful command does not silently erase an unresolved claim.

Check: compare original and retained criteria with closure evidence and linked residual work.
